**ADR 0001: stack for the backend, frontend and data layer.** Status: accepted.

## Context

The application lets employees clock in and out and lets managers manage teams and view KPIs (hours worked, lateness, averages), so date and time handling and aggregation matter. Fixed constraints: Docker and Docker Compose for deployment, GitHub Actions for CI, JWT access tokens with rotating refresh tokens, and Microsoft OAuth 2.0.

## Criteria

Weights were fixed before scoring. Scores run from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). The weighted total is the sum of score times weight, with a maximum of 5 x 16 = 80.

| ID | Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- | :-: |
| C1 | Team experience | 3 |
| C2 | Ecosystem quality (OAuth 2.0 providers, charting libraries) | 3 |
| C3 | Date and time handling and aggregation | 3 |
| C4 | Docker deployment complexity (higher is simpler) | 2 |
| C5 | Community and long-term viability | 2 |
| C6 | Learning value | 1 |
| C7 | Type safety and developer experience | 2 |

## Options

### Backend

| Option | C1 (x3) | C2 (x3) | C3 (x3) | C4 (x2) | C5 (x2) | C6 (x1) | C7 (x2) | Total /80 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bun and Fastify 5 (TypeScript) | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **69** |
| Python FastAPI | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 | **63** |
| Java Spring Boot | 2 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **61** |

Bun and Fastify lead on team experience, ecosystem fit and type safety. FastAPI is strongest on data handling but costs a language switch. Spring Boot is mature but heavy for the team and the Docker footprint. Gin is lean but has a thinner OAuth and ORM ecosystem.

### Data access (all on PostgreSQL)

| Option | C1 (x3) | C2 (x3) | C3 (x3) | C4 (x2) | C5 (x2) | C6 (x1) | C7 (x2) | Total /80 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PostgreSQL and Drizzle | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **66** |
| PostgreSQL and Prisma | 3 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **65** |
| PostgreSQL and TypeORM | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | **49** |

Drizzle and Prisma are close. Drizzle gives SQL-level control over date truncation and aggregation, a lighter image and no code-generation step. Prisma is the fallback. TypeORM trails on type safety and maintenance momentum.

## Decision

Use TypeScript on Bun with Fastify 5 for the backend, React 19 with Vite for the frontend, and PostgreSQL with Drizzle ORM for data.

Two criteria drove the decision. Date and time handling and aggregation (weight 3): clock-in records, lateness and averages need reliable timestamps, time zones and SQL grouping, which PostgreSQL and Drizzle's typed queries handle well, and recharts gives managers a mature charting layer. Team experience (weight 3): the team already knows TypeScript, and one language with shared types across API and SPA reduces onboarding time and integration bugs.

The data is relational by nature: users belong to teams, teams have managers and clocks reference users. Joins, constraints and KPI aggregate queries are simpler in SQL than in a document store, so a relational database was chosen over MongoDB.

## Consequences

- Bun's ecosystem is younger than Node.js. Some libraries may behave differently and its long-term support is less proven. Mitigations: avoid Bun-only APIs where practical, pin Bun and dependency versions, run CI on Bun, and keep the option to switch the Docker base image to Node.js, which should need only a Dockerfile change and a regenerated lockfile.
- Drizzle requires explicit migrations (`db:generate`) committed to the repository.
- Validation exists twice (JSON Schema on the API, zod in the SPA). The OpenAPI document and the SDK keep the contract in one place.
